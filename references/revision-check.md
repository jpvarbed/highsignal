# Check what the revision says

Compare each changed passage with the source. The revision should still make
every distinct claim in the original. Every claim in the revision should
follow from the source. Use surrounding context to settle what a phrase refers
to or to make the same point concrete. Adding true details from nearby text
doesn't make up for losing a claim in the edited passage.

Check for changed force as well as changed facts. “Built to reduce retries”
states a purpose. “Reduces retries” states a result. Preserve the key verb
when replacing it changes what happened, what was intended, or what the
author wants to compare. Keep distinct speakers' claims and disagreements
attributed to the right people. If the smallest edit
that fixes the prose still changes the claim, keep the wording and raise the
substantive edit separately for the author to decide.

For a dense passage, write the old claim next to the new one. Check who did
what, what was counted, what it was compared with, and when the claim holds.
Keep the original uncertainty and check any change to words such as *only*,
*all*, *most*, and *might*. Verify quotations, code, exact names, and link
targets against the source.

Check the explanation of the edit as well as the draft. “If” gives a condition
that is enough. “Only if” makes it necessary. Likewise, choosing to join a
program and having the option to leave it are two different facts.

In Declauding's example, “the format that most invites staged reveals” became
“invites staged reveals more than most formats do.” Both use *most*.
Only the original says that format ranks first. Compare what each sentence
commits the author to. [Declauding's preservation notes](https://github.com/oaustegard/claude-skills/blob/declauding-v0.13.0/declauding/references/preservation.md)

| When a pattern looks suspicious | Keep it when it does this work |
| --- | --- |
| A contrast | Rules out a possibility the reader has reason to consider. |
| A condition or hedge | Says when the finding holds or how sure the writer is. |
| A short paragraph | Marks a real change of subject, time, or direction. |
| A fragment | Works as a note, headline, caption, or deliberate beat in the writer's medium. |
| A term that sounds formal | Names a specific thing the reader or system already uses. |

Keep the exact name when it helps the reader act. If the interface calls it
**Retry limit**, use that label instead of “the recovery setting.” If the
draft says “it resets after 24 hours” and could mean either the counter or
the job, name which one resets once the source settles it.
