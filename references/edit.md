# Edit the argument

1. Read the whole draft. Write down the claim in one sentence, who needs to hear it,
   and what they should understand or do. Identify the evidence for the claim and
   any condition that limits it. Keep denominators and comparison groups with the
   results they explain.
2. Follow the argument as a reader. Find the first place you had to guess what a
   sentence referred to, why evidence supported a claim, or why a paragraph was
   there. Quote the words that caused the problem. Check for a missing premise
   or another plausible explanation. When evidence conflicts, name both pieces
   and ask which source, definition or condition resolves the conflict.
3. Try the fix in prose. Move a buried point, connect evidence to the claim, or
   give a necessary distinction its own sentence. Rewrite the affected passage
   in context. If the evidence supports only a narrower claim, offer that
   wording explicitly and say what would justify the stronger one. Leave
   factual decisions for the author when the draft cannot settle them.
4. Use the [revision check](revision-check.md). Confirm the reader now reaches
   the intended point. Keep good lines as written.

Give the author three things, in this order:

- **What I think you're saying.** State the argument as you understand it.
- **Where I lose the thread.** Say where and why, with an exact quote. Focus on
  the issues that affect the reader's understanding.
- **What I'd change.** Give a clean revision of the draft or requested passage.
  Then show the consequential choices as explicit **Before** and **After** pairs
  with a reason for each. Include at least one pair whenever you propose new
  wording. Under **Before**, quote the complete source sentence or exact selected
  span. Under **After**, give the matching replacement. For a move, name the new
  location. For a cut, identify what the remaining text still conveys.

For example, a clean revision may start with a deadline. Show the change separately.

- **Before** “There is a form you need to send us by Friday.”
- **After** “Send us the form by Friday.”
- **Why** Put the action and deadline in the same direct instruction.

For feedback only, give exact replacements for the affected passages and leave
the source alone. For an authorized file edit, apply the revision and report the
consequential changes. If you propose a different position or recommendation,
show the author the option before applying it. In Google Docs, give a clean
revision or apply direct edits if requested. Use Suggesting mode or comments
only if the author asks.

For a selection, read the surrounding text and show replacements for the
selection alone. If the full fix needs a change elsewhere, describe it separately.

If a contradiction prevents a faithful revision, flag it and ask what resolves
it. When only one passage is blocked, give a clean revision of the rest and
mark the gap outside the copy. If a replacement depends on one reading of an
ambiguous phrase, say what you assumed before the replacement. If the draft
already works, say what works and leave it alone.
