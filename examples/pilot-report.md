# Edit an inference in a pilot report

Draft

> Twelve people came to the first workshop at the community center. We held
> the second workshop at the library and put a sign by the front door.
> Twenty-four people came.
> The sign doubled attendance, so we should put one up for every workshop.

**What I think you're saying.** You think the sign helped people find the
workshop, and you want to make it standard.

**Where I lose the thread.** “The sign doubled attendance” gives the sign all
the credit. You also moved the workshop to the library. The two counts don't
tell me which change helped.

**What I'd change.** Report the counts and propose a way to check the sign's
effect.

> Attendance rose from 12 at the community center to 24 at the library, where
> we put a sign by the front door. I'd compare attendance at workshops in the
> same room with and without the sign before making it standard.

The original says “The sign doubled attendance, so we should put one up
for every workshop.” The revision gives the author a narrower claim and a
different recommendation to consider. Comparable workshops with the room,
schedule, and promotion held steady would make the case for the sign stronger.
