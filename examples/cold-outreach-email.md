# Example: cold outreach to an expert or investor

Emailing someone whose talk or work you admire, to ask for their read on a problem
(often sales / go-to-market) and maybe a door into their program or firm. The genre has
two built-in failure modes: generic praise that reads as a mass email, and burying the ask
underneath it.

## Before (typical draft)

> Hi [Name],
>
> I thought you gave one of the most useful talks at [Conference]. I'm a big believer in
> [their theme] — it's the whole thesis for what I'm building. My hardest problem: sales.
> I'd love your take on how to sell this idea.
>
> I'm building a solution to let companies [dense description of what it does]. It's
> self-serve and people can buy as much as they want.
>
> I'm going to apply to [Program] in the fall (link) and self-run, but wondering if [Firm]
> has something similar.
>
> Thanks again for the talk!

## Tells

- **Generic praise** — "one of the most useful talks" reads as a mass email and proves
  nothing. Name the *specific* thing they said; one concrete detail proves you were in the room.
- **Assertion with no content** — "I'm a big believer in [theme]." Show the belief through
  what you built; don't claim it.
- **Vague where it counts** — "sales", "buy as much as you want." Say self-serve and
  usage-based; say who the first buyer is.
- **Idea, not traction** — "I'm building a solution to let companies…" If you've built the
  thing, lead in the present tense: "I've built X that does Y."
- **Two tangled asks** — the GTM question and the program question blur together. Split them.
- **Em-dash overuse** — "[theme] — it's the whole thesis". One em dash in an email is fine;
  three or four is an AI tell. A period or comma reads more human. (avoid-ai-writing flags
  more than ~1 per 100 words.)
- **Label-colon throat-clear** — "My hardest problem: sales." The colon fakes a beat before a
  one-word payoff. Just write the sentence: "Sales is my hardest problem."

## After

> Hi [Name],
>
> [The specific point they made] at [Conference] stuck with me. It shaped how I'm thinking
> about what I'm building.
>
> I'm building [one concrete line, present tense if it exists]. It's self-serve and usage-based.
>
> The product I can build. Selling it is my weak spot, which is exactly what your talk was about.
>
> Two things I'd value your read on:
> - How would you get a self-serve motion like this off the ground? Who's the first buyer?
> - I'm applying to [Program] this fall and running it solo in the meantime (link). Does
>   [Firm] have anything similar?
>
> Thanks again for the talk.
> [You]

## The genre move

Lead with their actual idea, not "great talk." "You said X, and it changed how I think about
Y" reads as *I was in the room and it moved me* — far stronger than praise. Then earn the
attention with one concrete proof of traction, make the ask singular and easy, and be honest
about the gap you want their help with. That honesty is the reason they're the right person
to ask.
